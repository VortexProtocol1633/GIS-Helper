About GIS Helper

What it does, how to use it, and who built it.

Overview What GIS Helper does

GIS Helper is a coordinate conversion and tactical-marking tool. Give it any one of four coordinate systems and it returns the other three, instantly, on an interactive map — then let you plot units, zones and order of battle directly onto that same map.

⇄ Latitude / Longitude

Decimal degrees (DD) and degrees / minutes / seconds (DMS), with N/S/E/W hemisphere.

⇄ MGRS / USNG grid

Military grid references at 6-digit (100 m), 8-digit (10 m) and 10-digit (1 m) precision.

⇄ ArcGIS XY (UTM)

Easting / Northing in metres, with the UTM zone number and hemisphere.

⇄ Place names

Search any city, address or landmark by name and get every format back.

Overview Tactical marking layer

Beyond conversion, GIS Helper doubles as a lightweight operational map. Plot friendly units, define zones, and share the whole picture with anyone on your team — no account, no server, no install.

⚑ Six unit types

Company HQ (flag), Support Platoon (armoured vehicle), SIGINT Platoon (antenna), ISR Platoon (drone), HUMINT Platoon (detective) and Special Task (star, diamond or circle) — plus any custom types you define yourself.

□ Polygon zones

Draw named, colour-coded zones with an automatic area calculation in m² or km². Each type of zone can carry its own name, colour and fill opacity.

⚖ Full unit details

Each deployment records commander, troop strength, vehicles, arms & ammo, and special equipment — all visible in the marker popup.

💾 Saved & shareable

Markings persist in your browser automatically, and can be exported to or imported from a JSON file so the whole team sees the same map.

Everything lives in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere. Use Export JSON file if you need to move markings to another machine.

Why Built for the field, not the form

Most online converters go one direction, quietly drop the hemisphere, and make you guess a UTM zone. GIS Helper converts everything at once — so a lat/lon from a phone, an MGRS from a field form, and an XY for ArcGIS all stay in sync on the same screen. Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.

Step 1 Enter a location

Type a value into the input box and either press Enter or click Locate & convert. The tool supports four input formats.

Place name

Any free-text search — cities, addresses, landmarks.

Statue of Liberty, New York Eiffel Tower

Latitude / Longitude

Decimal degrees or DMS, with or without hemisphere letters.

40.6892, -74.0445 40° 41' 21" N, 74° 02' 40" W

MGRS / USNG grid

6-digit (100 m), 8-digit (10 m) or 10-digit (1 m) precision.

18T WL 807 046 18T WL 80735 04695

ArcGIS XY (UTM)

Easting / Northing in metres. Type the zone in the box next to the format menu, or include it inline.

580736, 4504695  + zone 18N 18N 580736 4504695

Auto-detect is on by default — the status pill in the top bar shows which format the tool has recognised as you type.

Step 2 Coordinate dashboard

Once a location is found, every format is shown at once — and every row is clickable, so you can copy a single value instead of the whole set.

  • Latitude / Longitude (DD) — decimal degrees with N/S/E/W.
  • Latitude / Longitude (DMS) — degrees, minutes, seconds.
  • ArcGIS XY — UTM Easting & Northing in metres, with zone & hemisphere.
  • MGRS 6-digit — a 100 m × 100 m square.
  • MGRS 8-digit — a 10 m × 10 m square.
  • MGRS 10-digit — a 1 m × 1 m square.

The Copy results as JSON button puts all values into your clipboard in a clean, machine-readable structure.

Step 3 Live cursor & the map

Move the mouse over the map and the values follow you in two places at once.

  • The Live cursor card in the sidebar updates with the current position.
  • The Cursor position panel at the bottom-right of the map mirrors the same values.
  • Click anywhere on the map to lock that point as the active location — the dashboard, marker and popup all update.
  • Drag the marker to fine-tune the position — the dashboard updates on release.

Map Controls & layers

  • Zoom — the +/− buttons on the top-left, or a scroll wheel / pinch.
  • Layer switcher — the stacked-layers button on the top-right opens the menu above with five base maps.
  • Scale bar & attribution — bottom-left, showing metric and imperial units.

Tips Little things worth knowing

  • Press Esc or click outside the window to close it.
  • Grid references without a space between digits — like 18TWL8073504695 — are also accepted.
  • If you paste XY without a zone, the tool will ask you to fill in the UTM zone field.
  • Hover the map first to preview a location, then click to confirm it.
  • Click any value in the Coordinate dashboard or Live cursor card to copy just that field.
  • The marker drops in with a bounce whenever a new point is selected.
  • Drag the dotted grip on a deployment row to set your order of battle — the legend follows it.
  • Use the copy button on a row to clone a unit, then edit the copy into a second element.
  • Reordering works from the keyboard too: focus a row, then Alt+↑ / ↓.
  • Click Manage types beside the Unit type label to add your own unit types — your names, icons and colours.
  • Custom unit types travel inside an exported JSON file, so importing shares the naming too.

Step 4 Deployments

Plot units on the map using one of six tactical unit types, each with its own icon, colour, and full equipment details. Open the card with the Deploy button in the top bar.

The six unit types

Each unit type is locked to its own icon set, so the map stays readable at a glance.

Company HQ

Flag icon · command element and headquarters.

Support Platoon

Armoured Vehicle icon · fires, logistics, transport.

SIGINT Platoon

Antenna / Signal Tower icon · signals & electronic intelligence.

ISR Platoon

Drone icon · aerial surveillance & reconnaissance.

HUMINT Platoon

Detective icon · human intelligence & liaison.

Special Task

Three icons available — pick whichever fits the element you are plotting:

Star Diamond Circle
  • Unit type — choose from the six types above. Changing the type resets the icon menu, the colour, and the live preview.
  • Icon — only the icons valid for the selected unit type are offered. Five types have a single fixed icon; Special Task offers all three shapes.
  • Color — each type has a sensible default (HQ teal, Support amber, SIGINT blue, ISR violet, HUMINT pink, Special Task red). Override it with the picker if you need to — e.g. to colour by task, not by unit.
  • Preview — the strip under the icon menu shows exactly what will appear on the map, updating live as you change anything.
  • Commander — name or callsign of the unit leader.
  • Troops — number of personnel.
  • Vehicles — free text: a count like 3, or a description like BTR-80 x2 + truck x1.
  • Arms & Ammo — weapons and ammunition, e.g. AKM x20, RPG-7 x3.
  • Special Equipment — night vision, optics, comms gear, etc.

Click Place on map to drop the marker at the currently selected map location. Markers are draggable — drag to reposition and the coordinates update automatically. Click a marker on the map, or a row in the list, to fly to it and read its full details in a popup.

Organising the list

Every placed unit appears as a row under the form. The list order is your order of battle — it drives the on-map legend and survives a page reload and an export, so you can sequence units however you like (by echelon, phase of the plan, or whoever is on the left of the map).

⋮⋮ Drag to reorder

Grab the dotted grip on the left of any row and drag it up or down. A coloured line shows whether the unit will land above or below the row you are hovering. Dragging near the top or bottom edge scrolls the list automatically.

⧉ Duplicate

Copies the unit — same type, icon, colour, commander, troops, vehicles, arms and equipment — and inserts the copy directly beneath the original, so you can then edit it into a second element. The copy is labelled “name (copy)” until you rename it.

⌫ Remove

Deletes the row and its map marker. There is no undo, so duplicate anything you might want back.

⌫︎ Keyboard

Focus a row with Tab, then use Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ to reorder, and Enter to fly to the unit on the map.

Drag-and-drop needs a mouse, so on a touch screen use the Alt+arrow keys or the keyboard shortcut instead. Reordering and duplication are saved immediately — there is no separate save step.

Creating your own unit types

The six types above are built in, but the list is not fixed. Click Manage types next to the Unit type label to add your own — useful when your organisation names things differently, or when you need an element the built-in set does not cover.

➕ Name

Up to 40 characters. Yours appear in a separate Your types group in the Unit type menu, below the built-in six.

Icons

Tick one to three from the same eight shapes the built-in types use. Several icons means the type can be drawn whichever way suits the element.

◓ Colour

Pick any colour. It becomes the type's default marker colour and the colour of its chip in the manager — though you can still override it per deployment.

✎ Edit & delete

Edit any time. Deleting a type that is still in use asks first, then moves those deployments to Company HQ — their commander, troops, vehicles, arms and equipment are all kept.

Custom types are stored in this browser and are written into the exported JSON file, so importing a teammate's file brings their unit types with it — nobody has to recreate them by hand.

All deployments are saved automatically to this browser. Use the Save & Share card to export them as a JSON file, or to import a teammate's markings and compare.

Step 5 Zones

Draw coloured polygon zones on the map — tactical sectors, perimeters, AO boundaries — with a name and calculated area.

  • Zone color — pick any colour; the fill and outline use this colour.
  • Zone name — a label like Alpha Sector or Perimiter A. If left blank, a default name is generated.
  • Fill opacity — how transparent the zone fill is (0.1 to 0.8).
  • Start drawing — click this button, then click on the map to add vertices. A dashed preview line follows your cursor.
  • Finish zone — click this button (or the Stop drawing button) to complete the polygon. You need at least 3 vertices.
  • Stop drawing — cancel the current drawing without saving.
  • Clear all zones — remove every zone from the map.

While drawing, double-click zoom is disabled so you can place vertices precisely. The zone's area (in m² or km²) is calculated automatically and shown in the popup and the zone list. Click a zone in the list to fly to its centre.

Zones are saved alongside deployments in localStorage, and travel with your copied and exported JSON data.

Step 6 Save & Share

Persist your markings locally and exchange them with others via JSON files.

💾 Save Op Data

One dialog for taking the plan away. Pick JSON file to download the complete session for a teammate, or PDF map sheet to open the print dialog on a single landscape sheet. For the PDF you tick what goes on it — map, scale bar, north arrow, deployments and zones. Unticking everything is refused rather than printing a blank page.

💾 Save to browser

Stores the map indicator, all deployments, zones and your custom unit types in this browser's localStorage. They reappear automatically when you return.

📂 Load from browser

Reloads the last saved session from localStorage, including the map indicator position.

📋 Copy all data

Puts the complete session JSON on your clipboard — the same payload the exported file contains. Paste it anywhere.

📦 Export JSON file

Downloads a gis-helper-data-YYYY-MM-DD.json file with everything — map indicator, deployments, zones, colours, details, areas.

📥 Import JSON file

Load a saved file from disk. You can also drag and drop a .json file anywhere onto the page.

Exported files can be emailed, shared on a drive, or posted online — anyone with the file can import it and see the same map. There is no server and no account: the file is the data, so a file you export on one machine opens identically on another. Files from older versions are also accepted (missing fields are filled in safely).

If the map already has data, importing asks whether to merge (add alongside what is there, keeping your current map indicator) or replace (clear everything and load the file exactly, including its map indicator). Cancelling leaves the map untouched. Your custom unit types are always merged in, never deleted.

Step 7 Print & image export

Turn the current plan into something you can hand over on paper or drop into a message.

🖨 Print report

Opens your browser’s print dialog with a one-page plan sheet: the map, then tables of every deployment and zone. Choose Save as PDF as the destination to get a PDF instead. This is the shortcut to the Save Op Data dialog’s PDF option, and it honours the same include ticks.

🖼 Export PNG

Downloads the same picture as a gis-helper-plan-YYYY-MM-DD.png — 1600×1000, ready to paste into a briefing or a report.

The picture frames every deployment, every zone and the map indicator, then numbers each deployment pin so it matches the row in the report table. It also carries a scale bar, a north arrow, and the base map’s attribution — which the OpenStreetMap, CARTO and OpenTopoMap licences require to stay visible.

No new dependencies and no server are involved: the image is drawn on a plain <canvas> in your browser. If the tile server refuses to serve tiles to a canvas (a browser security restriction that varies by provider), the export quietly falls back to a schematic plan — a coordinate grid with the same zones, pins and labels, just without the street imagery. You will be told which one you got.

Export options

These sit above the two export buttons and are remembered in this browser.

  • Frame — what the picture frames: everything, whatever the map is currently showing, deployments only, zones only, or just the map indicator. If you pick a preset that has nothing in it, you are told what is missing rather than given a blank sheet.
  • Scale bar — metric, imperial, both, or hidden. Imperial uses feet and miles.
  • Rotate to fit — off means north-up, which is also the default. Turn it on and the plan is spun onto its longest axis, so a long or diagonal plan fills the frame instead of leaving empty corners. The north arrow turns with it and the caption states the applied angle. A plan that already runs along an axis is left alone.
  • Plan title and Plan date — optional. They print in the report header, so you can label a sheet with an exercise name or an operation date. They are not burned into the PNG.

Plans of up to about sixteen rows of data stay on a single sheet: the tables tighten up and the map gives up the space they need. Beyond that the sheet runs onto a second page rather than shrinking the map until it is unreadable.

Engineer by training · designer by stubbornness

Hi — I'm the person behind GIS Helper. I did not go to design school; I studied at MIST (Military Institute of Science and Technology, Bangladesh), which taught me a lot about engineering and approximately nothing about colour theory. Everything you see here is self-taught, mildly obsessive, hobbyist-grade web design. The four themes were mostly me asking "does this mint go with this teal?" and then reloading the page forty times.

What I do know is the special kind of tedium that comes from needing one coordinate in three different formats at once. Someone hands you a lat/lon, the field form wants MGRS, the GIS software wants UTM Easting and Northing, and the only converter you can find online goes one direction, drops the hemisphere, and asks you to pick a UTM zone from a dropdown you're not sure about. Then you're counting grid digits by hand at 2 a.m. hoping you didn't shave off a zero. One day I got fed up enough to build a tool instead of complaining about it.

GIS Helper takes whatever you've got — a place name, a lat/lon pair, a 6- or 8-digit grid reference, a raw XY — and converts it into every other format at once, on a map, live. Like a very patient GIS analyst who never mixes up their eastings and northings.

Then it grew a second job. Somewhere between planning a rehearsal and actually drawing it on a sheet of acetate, I realised I was doing the same thing twice: pinning down where everything is, and then separately what is there. So I gave it a tactical marking layer — six unit types with their own icons (Company HQ, Support, SIGINT, ISR, HUMINT and Special Task), polygon zones that calculate their own area, and a save/export button so the plan leaves my laptop in a form other people can actually open.

No accounts, no server, no database. Everything is stored in your browser, and a single JSON file carries the whole order of battle to whoever needs it. It is the smallest tool that does the job, which is roughly the only kind of tool I trust with information nobody should have to upload to a stranger's database.

It started as a weekend fix for my own field notes and quietly turned into… this. I hope it saves you as much time (and head-scratching) as it's saved me.
